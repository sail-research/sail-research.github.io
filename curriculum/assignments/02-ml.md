# Assignment: a reproducible supervised-learning comparison

## Objective and scope

Build a leakage-safe baseline and test one neural-model design choice. Use the bundled digits or another public/synthetic dataset approved for the same learning objective. Estimated effort: 5–8 hours after the module. A negative result can earn full credit.

## Required experiment

Define the prediction event and data provenance. Save train/validation/test indices before preprocessing. Fit all learned transforms on training only. Include a majority baseline, a linear baseline and one neural model. Declare the tuning grid, selection metric, tie rule and maximum budget before final test evaluation.

Choose one ablation: hidden width, dropout, a small CNN versus MLP, or another clearly isolated design choice. Keep other settings fixed or explain unavoidable differences. Use at least three training seeds with a fixed split when feasible; if changing split seeds, label that variation separately. Do not claim equal compute merely because epoch counts match.

Verify tensor/target shapes, finite gradients, parameter updates, tiny-batch fitting and checkpoint restoration. Use evaluation mode correctly and aggregate losses over examples. Preserve all validation candidates and evaluate the selected configurations on test only after freezing the choice.

## Required analysis

Report accuracy, macro F1, class supports and confusion matrices. Include training/validation histories, per-seed results and a paired comparison when appropriate. Analyze at least five changed or failed predictions selected by a documented rule. State whether the observed effect is practically meaningful and which populations/settings remain untested.

## Deliverables

Provide runnable code/notebook, environment, data/split manifest, configuration/search history, selected checkpoint, raw predictions/metrics, plots and a 2–4 page report. Include commands for both a smoke check and the main experiment. The report must connect the claimed improvement or null result to a specific comparison, not merely list architecture scores.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Points | Full-credit evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Data and split validity | 20 | Prediction-time features, no leakage, saved provenance/indices |
| Baselines and controlled protocol | 20 | Appropriate comparators, declared selection and budget |
| Training implementation | 20 | Gradient/mode/averaging checks and checkpoint replay |
| Evaluation and error analysis | 25 | Correct metrics, seed evidence, failures and bounded claims |
| Artifacts and explanation | 15 | Reproducible command, complete outputs and readable report |

Essential gates: no test-based selection presented as held out, no input/label misalignment, valid model/evaluation modes and authentic recoverable results. Pass recommends at least 70 with gates; Excellent at least 90 plus a well-designed extension and a convincing explanation of alternative interpretations. Reviewer outcomes remain explicit rather than automatically calculated by the website.

## Self-review and reviewer guidance

Recompute one confusion matrix and metric independently from saved predictions. Ask whether the selected checkpoint truly came from validation. Reviewers should inspect a failed example and the full search table, not only the best score. Request Revision for repairable protocol/documentation errors before the deadline, naming the exact evidence needed. Do not penalize a correct finding that a simpler baseline performs as well as the neural model.
